Day 25 · Proposal 24 · Section X · Policy 27

Weekend Banking

Can Britain make Saturday and Sunday ordinary payment days?

Banking Payments Productivity Public scrutiny

The core idea

It has never made intuitive sense that a payment created digitally can still wait for Monday simply because it was scheduled for a weekend.

For households, workers, businesses and government, that delay can affect cash flow, certainty and access to money already due.

Proposal 24 asks whether the United Kingdom’s bulk and scheduled payment systems could be modernised so that Bacs Direct Credits, salaries, business payments and government payments can be credited on Saturdays and Sundays.

A citizen-led proposal: this project does not claim that every technical or institutional answer is already known. Publication is intended to test whether the objective is feasible, what would need to change, what it would cost and which risks must be protected against.
The weekend payment question

Why should a digital payment wait for Monday?

What happens now?

Bacs Direct Credit

The established bulk-payment rail used extensively for wages, benefits and business payments operates through a three-day processing cycle and a working-day calendar.

Faster Payments

A different UK payment rail already operates day and night, 365 days a year. Eligible transfers are normally available almost immediately, subject to participant limits and checks.

Salary and government files

Large scheduled payment files are commonly submitted through Bacs because it is established, predictable and designed for high-volume processing.

The settlement layer

Customer-facing payments depend on underlying interbank settlement, liquidity, reconciliation, operational support and scheme rules.

Some UK payments already operate continuously. The question is whether bulk and scheduled payments can safely become genuinely seven-day too.

Why does the working-day timetable exist?

The weekend pause is not simply caused by bank branches being closed. Bacs was built as a coordinated batch-clearing system. Moving it to seven-day processing could affect every participating bank, employer, government body, payroll provider and software platform connected to the scheme.

Batch efficiency

Files containing many payments are submitted, validated and processed together, supporting reliable and relatively low-cost high-volume payments.

Settlement and liquidity

Banks must hold and move sufficient funds between institutions. Wider hours change when liquidity and settlement support are required.

Reconciliation and returns

Participants need consistent processes for corrections, rejected instructions, account changes, reports and payment investigations.

Operational support

Seven-day processing may require weekend staffing, incident management, customer support and coordinated service standards.

Maintenance and resilience

Continuous availability must still allow safe upgrades, testing, recovery and contingency arrangements.

Rules and software

Payment calendars are embedded in contracts, payroll timetables, treasury processes, accounting systems and banking software.

The policy direction

Weekend crediting

Make it possible for eligible salaries, benefits, business payments and government payments to credit on Saturdays and Sundays.

Bacs modernisation

Examine whether the existing Bacs cycle could add weekend processing without weakening reliability or affordability.

Alternative routing

Test whether suitable bulk payments could use, or be bridged to, infrastructure that already supports continuous payment.

Published evidence

Require transparent assessment of costs, benefits, risks, timelines and impacts before any national commitment is finalised.

Proposed direction—not a settled mechanism: set the objective of seven-day crediting, then allow technical evidence to determine the safest and most proportionate route.

Possible routes for scrutiny

The destination may be clear while the mechanism remains open. These are options to investigate, not instructions presented as technically settled.

Option A — Extend Bacs

Develop seven-day submission, processing and settlement days within the existing Bacs scheme.

Option B — Use Faster Payments

Allow eligible scheduled or bulk credits to use real-time infrastructure where capacity, cost, controls and recipient reach permit.

Option C — Weekend value-dating

Explore whether receiving institutions could make verified funds available on the intended weekend date before the legacy cycle completes.

Option D — New bulk capability

Consider a modern bulk-payment service or overlay designed around seven-day availability and richer payment information.

What must not be compromised

  • Security and fraud prevention: effective authentication, screening, intervention and reimbursement protections must remain in place.
  • System resilience: extended operation must include safe maintenance, incident response, recovery and contingency arrangements.
  • Liquidity and settlement: banks must be able to fund obligations and manage exposure throughout any extended settlement window.
  • Affordability: modernisation costs must be transparent and must not unfairly exclude smaller employers, charities or public bodies.
  • Access and inclusion: people who rely on cash, branches or assisted banking must not be left behind.
  • Workforce fairness: extended financial operations require predictable staffing, adequate rest, fair reward and realistic specialist cover.

Questions for scrutiny

  1. Which technical, legal or operational rules currently prevent Bacs Direct Credits from settling or crediting at weekends?
  2. Could Bacs itself process seven days a week, or would eligible payments be better routed through Faster Payments or a new bulk-payment service?
  3. What capacity, liquidity and settlement changes would banks and the Bank of England need to support?
  4. Would seven-day processing materially improve household cash flow, business productivity and public-service delivery?
  5. What would implementation cost banks, employers, government, payroll providers and smaller organisations?
  6. How should fraud checks, mistaken payments, recalls, returns and customer complaints operate during weekends?
  7. Could implementation be phased by payment type or institution?
  8. What measurable pilot would establish whether the benefits justify the cost and complexity?
Core commitment

Commission an independent feasibility review, publish the costs, risks, options and timetable, and pilot the safest workable route before national implementation.

The intended destination

Money due on a weekend should not be delayed solely because the calendar says it is not a banking day.

Whether achieving that objective requires reforming Bacs, using existing real-time rails differently or building new capability must be determined through evidence and expert scrutiny.
