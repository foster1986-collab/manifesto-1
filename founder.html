Why I wrote Manifesto 1.0

I wrote Manifesto 1.0 because I believe Britain needs a bigger conversation about the kind of country it wants to become over the next decade.

Our political debate can become trapped between familiar choices: more state or less state, higher taxes or lower taxes, greater security or greater individual responsibility. I wanted to explore whether those objectives could be brought together into a different settlement.

The result is Manifesto 1.0: a deliberately ambitious programme covering income security, work, taxation, housing, health, energy, defence, infrastructure, welfare and long-term economic growth.

An open proposal

I don't expect everyone to agree with Manifesto 1.0. I want people to challenge it, test it and improve it.

Some of the proposals are deliberately provocative. The Universal Standard Income, the 16-hour participation principle, abolition of employee National Insurance, cannabis regulation, major nuclear expansion and the wider programme are intended to start a serious argument about Britain's future.

Manifesto 1.0 is called 1.0 for a reason. It is a starting point, not a claim to have every answer.
