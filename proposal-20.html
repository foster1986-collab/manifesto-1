Day 21 · Proposal 20 · Section VIII · Policy 23

18–21 Education and Training Bursary

Supporting young people who continue in approved education or training—so study, skills and opportunity remain financially viable.

Proposal 18: 21-Year Guarantee Proposal 19: Military Route Policy index

The core idea

Part VIII of Manifesto 1.1 is built around a simple principle: no young person should be left without a route to learn, gain skills, build experience and move towards adult working life.

Under Proposal 20, every 18–21-year-old participating in approved education or training would receive a government bursary. Its purpose is to support young people who continue studying or training and to make that choice financially viable.

One guarantee, different routes

The bursary is specifically for 18–21-year-olds who remain in approved education or training. Young people taking other routes within the wider 21-Year Guarantee would receive income through those routes instead.

Choosing to continue in education or training should not mean being the only young person within the guarantee without meaningful financial support.

The master manifesto does not yet specify the bursary amount, payment frequency, qualifying courses, household-income rules, treatment of existing student support or detailed administration. Those are implementation questions for scrutiny rather than details to invent at this stage.

Opportunity should come with support

The 21-Year Guarantee is intended to give every young person a credible pathway: education, training, employment, an apprenticeship or the voluntary 18–21 Military Route. The routes are different, but the underlying objective is the same—skills, experience and opportunity before the age of 21.

For those who choose approved education or training, the bursary is the financial-support mechanism. It is intended both to support participation and to incentivise young people to continue developing their knowledge and skills.

Why a bursary?

Continuing education or training can carry everyday costs while delaying or limiting access to full wages. The policy therefore proposes a government bursary so that remaining in an approved learning route is more financially viable.

Relationship with the previous proposals

Proposal 18 — The 21-Year Guarantee sets the wider ambition that young people remain connected to education, training, employment, an apprenticeship or military service until 21. The exact “required” versus “expected” wording remains unresolved in the working master.

Proposal 19 — The 18–21 Military Route offers a voluntary three-year Armed Forces option with salary, pension contributions, accommodation or support, military training, technical qualifications and transferable civilian skills, plus the opportunity at 21 to leave with recognised qualifications and employment support.

Proposal 20 — The Education and Training Bursary completes the financial-support side for young people in approved education or training.

Questions for scrutiny

  • What level should the bursary be set at to provide meaningful support without creating unsustainable cost?
  • Which forms of education and training should qualify as approved routes?
  • Should the bursary be universal for qualifying 18–21-year-olds, or vary by household income or circumstances?
  • How should it interact with existing student finance, benefits and other education support?
  • Should payment depend on attendance, participation or satisfactory progress—and, if so, how should that be assessed fairly?
  • How should young people moving between education, training, employment, apprenticeships and the military route be treated?
  • What safeguards are needed when education or training is interrupted by illness, disability, caring responsibilities or other circumstances?
  • What would the policy cost at different bursary levels and participation rates?

Core commitment

Every 18–21-year-old participating in approved education or training would receive a government bursary, while those in employment, apprenticeships or the Armed Forces would instead receive wages or salary.

The aim is not simply to keep young people occupied until 21. It is to ensure that every young person has the chance to study, develop skills, gain experience and build a route towards opportunity—and that choosing education or training is financially supported.
