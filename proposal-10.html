Planning Reform

End Planning Paralysis. A national housing and infrastructure test, a continuing role for local planning authorities and a final central-government power of intervention for repeatedly blocked nationally significant projects.

Prevent indefinite blockage of nationally important development while retaining enforceable standards for infrastructure, design and environmental obligations.

1. End Planning Paralysis

Manifesto 1.1 proposes a planning framework that combines a major local-authority role with a national route for qualifying housing and infrastructure projects.

The master sets out the commitment as follows:

Local planning authorities will retain a major role, but central government will retain a final power of intervention where nationally significant housing or infrastructure projects are repeatedly blocked. A national housing and infrastructure test will create a presumption in favour of approval for qualifying projects, while maintaining enforceable standards for infrastructure, design and environmental obligations. The objective is to prevent indefinite blockage of nationally important development while retaining those standards.

2. How planning would work

  • A continuing local role. Local planning authorities retain a major role in the proposed system.
  • A national housing and infrastructure test. Projects that qualify under the test would benefit from a presumption in favour of approval, alongside enforceable standards.
  • A final intervention power. Central government retains a final power of intervention where nationally significant housing or infrastructure projects are repeatedly blocked.

The purpose is to provide a route through repeated blockage for nationally important development while keeping the standards set out in the manifesto.

3. Keep enforceable standards

The proposed presumption in favour of approval is tied to qualifying projects. The commitment expressly maintains enforceable standards in three areas:

  • Infrastructure.
  • Design.
  • Environmental obligations.

These safeguards form part of the planning promise itself.

4. How this relates to the housing programme

Day 10 · Revised Proposal 09 sets out the ambition for one million additional homes over ten years through mixed tenure, with public and social delivery alongside conditional private-sector incentives.

That housing release already includes faster planning routes for schemes meeting national design, infrastructure and delivery tests. It also requires large housing schemes to be planned with transport, schools, primary care, utilities, drainage, digital connectivity and public space.

This separate planning proposal sets out the wider decision-making framework: the local-authority role, the national housing and infrastructure test and the final central-government intervention power.
