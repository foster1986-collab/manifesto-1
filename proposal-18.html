Manifesto 1.1 — A New Settlement for Britain
Day 19 · Proposal 18 · Section VIII

The 21-Year Guarantee

No one under 21 left behind.
Core purpose:
Ensure every young person reaches 21 through education, approved training, employment, an apprenticeship or a dedicated three-year Armed Forces route — with financial support and a credible path into adult working life.

Why this proposal exists

This proposal was prompted by repeated public discussion and media reporting about the number of young people who are not in education, employment or training — often described as NEETs.

The proposed response is broader than a single employment programme. It creates a guarantee that every young person has a recognised route through to their 21st birthday.

The core commitment

Under Proposal 18, every young person would be required to remain in an approved route until their 21st birthday, subject to appropriate exemptions, safeguarding and hardship protections.

Education

Continued academic study supported by a government bursary.

Approved Training

Vocational or skills-based training supported by a government bursary.

Employment

Paid employment providing income, experience and progression.

Apprenticeship

Paid work combined with recognised training and qualifications.

18–21 Military Route

A three-year Armed Forces route providing salary, pension contributions, training, qualifications and transferable skills.

Financial security in every route

The guarantee combines responsibility with a genuine offer. If participation is compulsory, every approved route must provide either income or meaningful financial support.

  • Young people remaining in approved education or training would receive a government bursary.
  • Those undertaking apprenticeships would receive a wage.
  • Those in employment would receive wages or salary.
  • Those choosing the military route would receive a salary and pension contributions.

The 18–21 Military Route

From age 18, a young person could choose a dedicated three-year Armed Forces programme.

The programme would provide:

  • a salary;
  • pension contributions;
  • accommodation and support;
  • military training;
  • technical and real-world qualifications;
  • transferable civilian skills; and
  • recognised qualifications and employment support for those choosing to leave at age 21.

The route is intended to prepare participants both for military service and for civilian working life.

Participation and benefits

Young people who are capable of participating but persistently refuse all reasonable approved routes would face restrictions on unemployment-related benefits.

The purpose would be to reinforce the participation requirement, not to abandon a young person in genuine hardship.

Detailed implementation would therefore need safeguards covering disability or illness, caring responsibilities, homelessness, safeguarding concerns, temporary crises, appeals and minimum hardship protection.

Responsibility must be matched by opportunity.
If participation is compulsory, meaningful routes must actually be available.

What the guarantee is intended to achieve

  • Reduce the number of young people reaching adulthood without education, work or recognised training.
  • Make education and approved training financially viable.
  • Ensure employment and apprenticeships provide earnings alongside experience and skills.
  • Create a structured military route with pay, pension contributions, qualifications and civilian transition support.
  • Give every young person a stronger financial and practical foundation at the beginning of adult working life.

Indicative fiscal position

Manifesto 1.1 Budget 3.1 currently provides an indicative annual envelope of £8–£10 billion for the wider Education / 21-Year Guarantee.

This remains a manifesto working estimate rather than an independently certified costing. The bursary level, eligible courses, military-route cost, administration, interaction with existing spending and any benefit savings would all require detailed modelling.

Questions for public scrutiny

  • Should participation until age 21 be a legal requirement?
  • How should that requirement be enforced?
  • Which circumstances should create a full or temporary exemption?
  • What level should the education and training bursary be?
  • Which education and training programmes should qualify?
  • How should benefit restrictions operate without creating homelessness, hunger or unsafe circumstances for vulnerable young people?
  • What alternatives should exist for young people who are medically or otherwise unsuitable for military service?
  • Which recognised civilian qualifications should the three-year military programme deliver?
  • How should success be measured — qualifications, sustained employment, earnings, wellbeing, NEET rates, or a combination?

Core commitment

No one under 21 left behind.

Require every young person to remain in education, approved training, employment, an apprenticeship or the 18–21 military route until their 21st birthday; attach financial support or earnings to each route; and ensure the system equips young people with greater security, skills, experience and a credible route into adult working life.
