DAY 40 · PROPOSAL 39 · PART XXII · POLICIES 43 & 44

Healthy Food Fund & Manufacturer Reformulation.

Reinvest food-tax receipts and reward healthier products.

The combined idea

Manifesto 1.1 combines two final food policies. Policy 43 would place supplementary taxation receipts from qualifying Red and Amber foods into a dedicated Healthy Food Fund.

Policy 44 would use the traffic-light classification and taxation system to encourage manufacturers to improve the nutritional composition of their products.

Together, the policies aim to recycle relevant receipts into healthier-food and public-health programmes while changing the commercial incentives facing the food industry.

Exact allocations, administrative costs, shortfall arrangements and detailed reclassification criteria remain open for modelling and scrutiny.

Use food-tax receipts to support healthier choices and reward healthier products.

What the Healthy Food Fund would support

Point-of-sale subsidies

Finance lower checkout prices for qualifying healthier foods.

Nutritional education

Support clear, practical education about food and nutrition.

School food

Fund programmes intended to improve food offered to children.

Public health

Support initiatives designed to improve diet-related health.

Healthy cooking

Help people develop the skills to prepare healthier meals.

Food access

Improve access to healthy food in deprived communities.

How the fund would operate

The source sets a rebalancing objective rather than promising a precisely revenue-neutral system. The primary purpose is to change food prices and consumption patterns, not to maximise tax receipts.

  • Collect: Place relevant supplementary receipts from qualifying Red and Amber foods into the dedicated fund.
  • Reinvest: Use the fund for healthy-food subsidies and the five other programmes named in Policy 43.
  • Review: Compare receipts with programme costs, administration, reach and the intended price effects.
  • Rebalance: If receipts materially exceed subsidy costs, review tax rates and increase subsidies or reduce the tax burden where appropriate.
The source preserves a rebalancing objective. Exact revenue neutrality is not asserted.

Design choices government must resolve

  • Allocation: Set transparent shares for subsidies, education, school food, public health, cooking and access.
  • Shortfalls: Define what happens when receipts are lower or more volatile than programme costs.
  • Administration: Model collection, distribution, audit and programme-management costs.
  • Governance: Establish oversight, published accounts, eligibility rules and conflict-of-interest safeguards.
  • Devolution: Clarify delivery and funding responsibilities across the United Kingdom.
  • Review triggers: Define when tax rates, subsidies, allocations or programmes should change.

Reformulating the food market

Policy 44 would use the traffic-light classification and taxation system to create a financial incentive for manufacturers to reformulate products.

Products that genuinely improve could qualify for reclassification where they meet the relevant criteria.

  • Reduce sugar: Encourage lower sugar content where reformulation is practical.
  • Reduce salt: Reward meaningful reductions in salt against published criteria.
  • Trans fats: Eliminate or reduce trans fats in qualifying products.
  • Saturated fat: Encourage reductions in saturated fat where appropriate.
  • Increase fibre: Reward products that improve their fibre content.
  • Protein quality: Encourage improvements in the quality of protein.
  • Less processing: Reduce unnecessary processing where a workable definition applies.
  • Nutritional value: Improve the product's overall nutritional composition.

Safeguards and success measures

  • Require independently verifiable nutritional changes before a product can be reclassified.
  • Prevent gaming through portion sizes, selective claims, substitution or misleading presentation.
  • Track movement between Green, Amber and Red classifications and the reasons for each change.
  • Measure whether reformulation improves products without raising prices or reducing access unfairly.
  • Publish fund receipts, allocations, administrative costs and programme outcomes.
  • Review health, equality, market and small-business effects alongside consumer purchasing patterns.
Change the incentives facing consumers and the entire food industry.

Questions for scrutiny

The combined proposal should be tested with public-health and nutrition specialists, schools, consumer and poverty groups, manufacturers, farmers, retailers, tax experts, local government and the devolved administrations.

  • Which food-tax receipts should enter the Healthy Food Fund, and how should they be accounted for?
  • How should funding be divided between subsidies and the five other stated programmes?
  • What governance, audit and public-reporting arrangements should protect the fund?
  • How should administration be financed without weakening the intended consumer benefit?
  • What happens when receipts fall below programme costs or vary substantially from year to year?
  • When should excess receipts trigger higher subsidies, lower tax rates or different allocations?
  • Which nutritional changes should qualify a product for reclassification?
  • How should improvements be measured across different product categories and serving sizes?
  • How can genuine reformulation be distinguished from gaming or misleading claims?
  • Could reformulation create unintended effects on price, taste, access or smaller manufacturers?
  • How should farming, imports, supply chains and domestic food production be considered?
  • Which price, health, equality, industry and fund outcomes should trigger revision or withdrawal?
Reinvest relevant receipts and reward food that becomes healthier.

The proposed direction

Proposal 39 would establish a dedicated Healthy Food Fund using relevant supplementary food-tax receipts and would create a financial incentive for manufacturers to improve products.

The fund would support point-of-sale subsidies, nutrition education, school food, public health, healthy cooking and food-access programmes in deprived communities.

Products that improve their nutritional composition could be reclassified where they meet the relevant criteria.

Before implementation, government must publish the fund's governance, allocations, administration, shortfall treatment, reformulation tests, audit arrangements and review triggers.

Source basis: Manifesto 1.1, Part XXII, Policies 43 and 44, “Food Taxation and Revenue Neutrality” and “Food Manufacturers and Reformulation”. Policy 43 establishes a Healthy Food Fund with six named uses and requires review where receipts materially exceed subsidy costs. Policy 44 sets eight reformulation objectives and allows reclassification where relevant criteria are met. The source does not fix fund allocations, administrative costs, shortfall treatment, governance, detailed reformulation thresholds or implementation dates; these remain open for modelling and scrutiny.
