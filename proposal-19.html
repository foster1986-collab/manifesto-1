A New Settlement for Britain
Day 20 · Proposal 19 · Section VIII · Policy 22

The 18–21 Military Route

A voluntary three-year Armed Forces route offering salary, training, recognised qualifications and transferable civilian skills.

The Core Idea

At 18, young people would have the option to join the Armed Forces for a three-year programme. At 21, they would have the opportunity to leave with recognised qualifications and employment support.

What the route provides

The working master sets out a clear package for young people choosing this route:

  • A salary
  • Pension contributions
  • Accommodation and support
  • Military training
  • Technical qualifications
  • Transferable civilian skills

Why it sits inside the 21-Year Guarantee

Policy 22 is one route within the wider under-21 settlement. The preceding policy says every young person will be expected to remain in education, training, employment, an apprenticeship or military service until their 21st birthday.

The military route therefore offers an additional pathway rather than replacing civilian education, training, apprenticeships or employment.

The wider 21-Year Guarantee still contains an unresolved wording question: the original Manifesto 1.0 used “required”, while Manifesto 1.1 uses “expected”. This proposal does not invent an enforcement mechanism or resolve that separate question.

From service to civilian opportunity

The route is designed around both service and what comes afterwards.

A three-year programme

The programme would begin at age 18. During that period, participants would receive the pay and support associated with the route while gaining military training, technical qualifications and skills intended to transfer into civilian employment.

At 21

At the end of the programme, participants would have the opportunity to leave the Armed Forces with recognised qualifications and employment support.

The working master does not require a participant to remain in military service beyond the three-year route.

What this proposal does not yet specify

The proposal sets out the route’s duration and core package, but it does not yet provide a complete implementation architecture.

It does not specify recruitment numbers, service allocation, detailed qualification frameworks, medical or fitness standards, training capacity, annual cost, safeguards for unsuitable applicants, or the precise form of civilian employment support.

Those details should be developed through evidence, operational testing and public scrutiny rather than invented at this stage.

Questions for scrutiny

A serious military route for young people has to work for the individual, the Armed Forces and the country.

  • How many places could the Armed Forces realistically provide each year without weakening training quality or operational readiness?
  • Which technical and vocational qualifications should be available, and how should they be recognised by civilian employers?
  • What recruitment, medical, fitness and suitability standards should apply?
  • How should accommodation, welfare, safeguarding and pastoral support work for 18–21-year-olds?
  • What should happen when a participant discovers that military service is unsuitable before completing the three years?
  • How should the route interact with apprenticeships, further education and other pathways under the 21-Year Guarantee?
  • What employment support should be guaranteed at 21, and how should transferable skills be evidenced to employers?
  • What would the programme cost at different participation levels, and what existing Armed Forces capacity would be required?

Core commitment

Offer young people, from age 18, a voluntary three-year Armed Forces route providing salary, pension contributions, accommodation/support, military training, technical qualifications and transferable civilian skills, with the opportunity at 21 to leave with recognised qualifications and employment support.

The principle is straightforward: if military service is offered as one pathway into adulthood, it should provide not only service to the country, but a credible foundation for the participant’s future.
